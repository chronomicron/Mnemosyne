# MindSteps Exercise Library

An evolving library of original, tablet-friendly games and exercises for **MindSteps from Mnemosyne**. The ideas draw on broad cognitive skill areas and familiar puzzle mechanics. Instructions, stimuli, images, sequences, scoring, and interface designs should be created for MindSteps rather than copied from a named assessment.

This is an idea catalog, not a fixed battery. Exercises can be selected and adjusted for the person, their interests, language, sensory needs, and comfort with a tablet. Each activity may involve more than one skill; the primary and secondary tags below are working design labels.

## Exercise design principles

- Make activities feel like approachable games, with clear instructions, large targets, readable contrast, simple navigation, and an easy way to pause or repeat directions.
- Give important instructions in both written and spoken form when appropriate. Include an easy replay, skip, or “I don't know” choice; speech recognition should allow reasonable pronunciation variation and have a tap-based alternative.
- Offer a practice round before recording a scored round. Allow different interaction methods where practical, such as tap-to-select as an alternative to dragging.
- Provide difficulty settings that can change one thing at a time: number of items, sequence length, delay, distractor similarity, number of rules, or audio pace.
- Keep a set of comparable alternate forms for repeated check-ins. Record the form and difficulty so a later result can be interpreted against the earlier task.
- Separate casual play from a repeatable check-in mode. Practice can use adaptive challenge and familiar feedback; check-ins should use recorded settings and balanced alternate content.
- Record task-level outcomes where useful: accuracy, correct-response time, omissions, false presses, corrections, retries, help/replays, completion time, and interaction mode. Keep timing anchored to known screen or audio events.
- Do not require a camera, microphone, drawing, or dragging to complete the whole suite. Provide touch-only or caregiver-assisted alternatives.
- For voice activities, support local processing where feasible, provide a tap-based alternative, and avoid retaining raw audio by default. For camera activities, activate the camera only for that activity and avoid saving room images by default.

## Suggested difficulty ladder

These are adjustable design levels, not age bands or a ranking of people.

1. **Warm-up:** familiar content, one rule, few choices, no timer, directions can be replayed.
2. **Steady:** more choices or a short sequence, with clear visual differences and no speed pressure.
3. **Challenge:** longer sequences, more similar distractors, a delayed response, or a rule change.
4. **Personal best:** optional timed play, longer multi-step plans, or two demands at once. Keep this mode optional and record its settings separately.

## A. Visual-spatial, construction, and object structure

1. **Which One Is a Bicycle?** Choose the structurally plausible bicycle from four or six bicycle-like drawings. Distractors can vary one feature at a time: frame connections, wheel placement, fork direction, or pedal/drive arrangement. *Measures:* choice, accuracy, latency, and distractor type.
2. **Build the Bicycle** — Drag or tap bicycle components into a complete frame outline. Begin with a few large parts; later levels add similar-looking parts or rotate a component. *Measures:* placements, misplacements, corrections, and time.
3. **Put the Tools Away** — Place tools into matching toolbox outlines. Later sets include tools with similar silhouettes or a partially filled box. *Measures:* match accuracy, placement order, errors, and completion time.
4. **Shadow Match** — Match an everyday object to its silhouette among several choices. Rotate or vary the viewpoint at higher levels. *Measures:* accuracy and decision time.
5. **Shape Turnaround** — Choose which shape is the same as a target after rotation. Increase rotation angle or add mirror-image distractors. *Measures:* accuracy, latency, and rotation level.
6. **Puzzle Tray** — Fit a small number of large, high-contrast pieces into a picture. Difficulty can grow from two pieces to more pieces or less obvious boundaries. *Measures:* correct placements, moves, undo actions, and time.
7. **Missing Tile** — Choose the piece that completes a simple visual pattern or picture grid. Patterns can use shape, position, number, or color. *Measures:* choice, accuracy, latency, and pattern type.
8. **Copy the Pattern** — Recreate a model by tapping colored/shape tiles in the same locations. Later rounds introduce a short delay before the model disappears. *Measures:* position accuracy, completion time, and revisions.
9. **Mirror It** — Select or build the reflected version of a simple arrangement. Start with one object and add objects or grid positions. *Measures:* spatial accuracy and response time.
10. **Find the Turned Picture** — Among several drawings, select the one that matches after rotation, with size and orientation changes. *Measures:* accuracy, latency, and error type.
11. **Trace a Route** — Follow a path on a simple map from home to a destination without crossing blocked areas. Later levels add one or two turns or a landmark rule. *Measures:* route accuracy, detours, corrections, and time.
12. **Picture Assembly** — Arrange image fragments into a coherent familiar object, such as a chair, flower, or bicycle. This is a piece-assembly variant of the bicycle recognition exercise. *Measures:* placements, errors, and completion time.
**A13. Set the Clock Hands** — Drag or tap clock hands to show a requested time. A separate variant places numbered tiles or icons on a blank clock or calendar layout. *Measures:* hand/number placement, corrections, response time, and interaction method.

**A14. Connect the Dots** — Tap numbered or lettered points in order to reveal a simple picture. A touch-only version can highlight the next point rather than requiring drawing. *Measures:* order errors, skipped points, corrections, and time.

**A15. Build a Block Shape** — Rotate and place large digital blocks to match a target cube or interlocking shape. Begin with a small number of pieces and clear snap points. *Measures:* placements, rotation attempts, errors, and completion time.

**A16. Draw a Familiar Object** — Draw a bicycle, house, cube, or another familiar object on a large touch canvas. Offer a component checklist and an alternative drag-and-drop construction mode. *Measures:* included parts, spatial relationships, stroke order, pauses, corrections, and completion time.

**A17. Copy the Figure** — Copy a simple original geometric design beside its model. Let the player use finger, stylus, or large shape stamps. *Measures:* component placement, orientation, overlap, stroke/placement order, and corrections.

**A18. Maze Trace** — Trace a wide, simple path from start to finish without crossing boundaries. Make the path wide enough for finger use and include a tap-through alternative. *Measures:* time, boundary contacts, backtracking, and path completion.

**A19. Find the Middle** — Tap the midpoint of a horizontal or vertical line, then try lines of different lengths and screen positions. *Measures:* signed midpoint error, response time, and missed-side pattern.

**A20. Spot the Difference** — Compare two similar original scenes and tap the objects or details that changed. Change one detail at a time in early levels. *Measures:* correct differences, false taps, search time, and location of missed items.

**A21. Find the Hidden Shape** — Locate a simple shape embedded among overlapping figures, or name an object shown from an unusual angle or with a partly missing outline. *Measures:* accuracy, response time, clue use, and item type.

**A22. Compare Size and Distance** — Choose which object appears larger or closer in a simple scene. Vary one visual cue at a time. *Measures:* choice, latency, and cue condition.

**A23. Left or Right?** — Select the requested side of the screen or a left/right-oriented object. Include mirrored layouts and simple practice examples. *Measures:* correct side, reversal errors, response time, and layout.

**A24. Quick Quantity Look** — Briefly show a small group of dots or objects and ask how many appeared, without counting one by one. Increase the set gradually. *Measures:* estimate, exact accuracy, response time, and set size.

**A25. Symbol-to-Number Key** — Show a small key linking symbols to numbers, then let the player enter the matching numbers for a row of symbols. *Measures:* correct mappings, omissions, substitutions, and completion time.

**A26. Complete the Symmetry** — Show half of a simple symmetrical picture and let the player build or choose its other half. *Measures:* matching positions, errors, and completion time.

## B. Visual attention and executive control

13. **Tap the Target Category** — Hear or read a target rule such as “Tap every fruit” and select matching pictures/words as they appear. Rotate targets among animals, fruits, tools, vehicles, or colors. *Measures:* hits, misses, false alarms, and response time per item.
14. **Listen for the Target** — Hear a spoken list of mixed categories and tap a large button only when the target category is named. Change the rule between rounds. *Measures:* hit rate, false presses, omissions, target response times, and rule-replay count.
15. **Now Switch** — Sort by color in one block and shape in another; an on-screen cue tells the player when the rule changes. *Measures:* accuracy before/after switches, switch cost, errors, and latency.
16. **Go / Wait** — Tap for one class of picture or sound, and wait for a different class. Use large buttons and a relaxed pace. *Measures:* correct taps, successful waits, false starts, misses, and reaction time.
17. **Picture Search** — Find all instances of a target in a tidy grid, such as every umbrella or every red flower. Increase grid size or visual similarity gradually. *Measures:* target hits, misses, non-target taps, scan time, and corrections.
18. **What Changed?** — Study a small group of objects, then identify the item that changed, moved, appeared, or disappeared. Change one property per level. *Measures:* accuracy, response time, and change type.
19. **Keep the Rule in Mind** — Display a simple rule (“choose the larger number” or “tap the blue animal”) and present items one by one. Occasionally repeat the rule. *Measures:* accuracy, prompts requested, response time, and rule-switch errors.
20. **Two Things at Once** — Listen for an animal word while also tapping a visual star, with the two streams paced gently. Add this only after each single-task version is comfortable. *Measures:* accuracy and timing in each stream and the change from single-task performance.
21. **Count the Visitors** — Tap whenever a chosen kind of object passes across the screen, then report how many appeared. *Measures:* target detections, count accuracy, and time to answer.
22. **Odd One Out** — Select the item that differs by a stated feature, such as “Which picture is not a vehicle?” or “Which shape has no curved edge?” *Measures:* accuracy, latency, and category/feature type.
23. **Follow the Route Rule** — Move a marker through a simple path while following one constraint, such as “visit the bakery before the park.” Add a second constraint at higher levels. *Measures:* rule adherence, extra moves, corrections, and time.
**B24. Alternating Trail** — Tap or connect a sequence that alternates two sets, such as 1-A-2-B-3-C. Start with a short trail and keep the next target visible as an optional hint. *Measures:* sequence errors, switching latency, corrections, and completion time.

**B25. Color or Word?** — Choose the ink color of a printed color word, ignoring what the word says. Use a non-reading symbol/color version as an alternative. *Measures:* accuracy, response time, conflict errors, and literacy mode.

**B26. Spoken Rule Change** — Sort or select items by one rule, then switch when a spoken instruction changes the rule (for example, color to shape). *Measures:* switch errors, response time before/after the change, and instruction replays.

**B27. Tower Planner** — Move colored disks or blocks between pegs to reach a target arrangement while following simple move rules. Begin with a few pieces. *Measures:* moves, rule errors, completed plan, and time.

**B28. Opposite the Arrow** — Tap the side opposite the direction of an arrow, then vary the cue or add a spoken rule. *Measures:* correct side, inhibition errors, response time, and rule version.

**B29. Two-Rule Go/No-Go** — Respond only to a combination such as red circles, and wait for all other combinations. Increase the number of visual properties gradually. *Measures:* hits, false presses, omissions, and response time by item type.

**B30. Spot the Mistake** — Find a deliberate inconsistency in a simple scene or short sentence (for example, a familiar object in an unusual place). *Measures:* detected errors, false alarms, response time, and error category.

**B31. Moving Target Follow** — Follow one target as it moves among a small number of stationary or moving objects, then select where it ended. *Measures:* tracking accuracy, missed transitions, response time, and number of distractors.

**B32. Simple Choice Reaction** — Tap a large left or right button depending on a single visual cue, or tap once when the screen changes. Keep a no-deadline version. *Measures:* response time distribution, wrong-side taps, and omissions.

**B33. Sustained Target Watch** — Watch a slow stream for a chosen target over a short interval, such as two minutes, with an easy stop control. *Measures:* hits, misses, false alarms, response-time drift, and duration completed.

## C. Memory and learning

24. **Repeat the Number Sequence** — Show or speak a short sequence and ask the player to repeat it by tapping number tiles or saying it aloud. Grow sequence length gradually. *Measures:* longest correct span, item-order errors, response time, and input mode.
25. **Backwards Numbers** — Present a short number sequence and ask for it in reverse. Use tap tiles or voice response. *Measures:* longest correct reverse span, order errors, and response time.
26. **Remember the Places** — Briefly show objects in positions on a small grid; ask the player to put them back. *Measures:* location accuracy, order accuracy when relevant, and completion time.
27. **Picture List Recall** — Show several distinct objects, then after a delay ask which ones were shown. Use free selection or recognition choices. *Measures:* immediate/delayed hits, false selections, and recall delay.
28. **Word List Recall** — Read a short list of everyday words and invite the player to say or select as many as they remember. Follow free recall with an optional category cue, then recognition choices. Use original rotating lists and consistent delay settings. *Measures:* recalled items at each stage, order if relevant, repetitions, intrusions, false recognition, and recall latency.
29. **Sound Sequence Replay** — Play a sequence of tones or familiar sounds; the player taps the same order on large sound tiles. *Measures:* longest correct sequence, order errors, and replay requests.
30. **Picture Story** — Show a short, original illustrated scene, then ask about details immediately or after another activity. Questions can be multiple choice or spoken. *Measures:* correct details, response time, and delay.
31. **Match the Pairs** — Reveal cards and match identical pictures, starting with a small board and adding pairs. Alternate sets can use animals, household objects, plants, or places. *Measures:* matched pairs, wrong turns, number of reveals, and time.
32. **Who Goes With Whom?** — Match baby animals to adults (cub/polar bear, calf/giraffe, foal/horse) or match objects to a familiar use. Other sets can include flag/country or food/country pairings. *Measures:* correct pairs, errors, time per pair, and revisions.
33. **Face and Name Friends** — Learn a few fictional or caregiver-approved names paired with portraits, then match them after a delay or say the name aloud. *Measures:* immediate/delayed pair accuracy, response time, and answer mode. Keep personalization optional.
34. **Remember What to Do Later** — At the start, ask the player to perform a simple action after a cue or after a short game, such as tapping a named icon when it appears. *Measures:* whether the intended action happens, timing, and prompts needed.
35. **Learn the Set** — Repeat learning and recall rounds for a small set of items. Plot correct items or learning efficiency across repetitions. Use equivalent forms across separate check-ins. *Measures:* acquisition across trials, delayed recall, and intrusions.
36. **Room-to-Object Match** — Learn which items belong in which room (mug/kitchen, pillow/bedroom, watering can/garden), then match after a delay or a distractor task. *Measures:* association accuracy, response time, and delay.
**C37. Spatial Sequence Span** — Light up a sequence of squares on a grid and ask the player to tap them back in order or reverse order. This differs from remembering object locations because the focus is on the sequence of positions. *Measures:* longest correct span, order errors, and response latency.

**C38. Paired-Word Learning** — Learn simple word pairs (such as table/chair or hand/glove), then recall the second word when shown the first. Use picture pairs as an alternative. *Measures:* acquisition across trials, cued recall, and response time.

**C39. Recall After a Distractor** — Learn a small set of words or pictures, complete a different activity, then recall the first set. Offer optional semantic cues and recognition choices after free recall. *Measures:* free recall, cued recall, recognition, intrusions, and delay.

**C40. Seen, Heard, or Both?** — After presenting items visually, audibly, or in both channels, ask where each item appeared. *Measures:* source accuracy, source-confusion errors, response time, and presentation mode.

**C41. Personal and Remote Memory Cards** — With the person's or caregiver's choice, use familiar people, places, or life events as conversation and recognition cards. Keep these items in a separate personalized set from repeatable general forms. *Measures:* recognition, recalled details, response time, and support used.

**C42. Procedural Sequence Practice** — Learn a short, repeatable tapping or button sequence over several rounds. Track how accurately and smoothly the sequence is performed as it becomes familiar. *Measures:* sequence accuracy, inter-tap timing, errors, and improvement across repetitions.

**C43. Memory Confidence Check** — After a recall or recognition round, invite the player to say how sure they feel or choose a confidence face. Compare confidence with actual responses as a separate metacognition measure. *Measures:* confidence choice and calibration against accuracy.

**C44. Remember the Route** — Watch a short route appear on a simple map, then retrace it after a brief pause or another activity. *Measures:* route accuracy, order errors, detours, delay, and time.

**C45. Personal History Cards** — With the person's choice and caregiver assistance, use familiar but non-sensitive prompts about places, occupations, or past events. Accept “skip” and record whether a cue was offered. *Measures:* response, latency, cue level, and whether the prompt was personalized.

## D. Reasoning, patterns, and planning

37. **Continue the Number Pattern** — Complete an original numeric sequence using one rule, such as adding a fixed amount or alternating two operations. Use touch choices or an on-screen number pad. *Measures:* accuracy, response time, and pattern type.
38. **Continue the Picture Pattern** — Complete a sequence of shapes, colors, or objects. Start with repeating patterns, then use alternating or growing patterns. *Measures:* accuracy and latency.
39. **Which Items Belong Together?** — Sort a set into categories (fruit/tools/animals, indoor/outdoor, things that roll/things that fly). Later levels use a less obvious but clearly stated relationship. *Measures:* grouping accuracy, time, and category-switch errors.
40. **Finish the Analogy** — Complete original familiar relationships with picture choices, such as “glove goes with hand as sock goes with ___.” *Measures:* accuracy, response time, and whether text or pictures were used.
41. **Put the Steps in Order** — Arrange pictures for an everyday process, such as preparing tea, mailing a letter, or planting a seed. *Measures:* order accuracy, moves, and time.
42. **Plan the Errand Route** — Choose an order for visiting places while respecting one or two simple constraints. Offer a map with few locations and clear labels. *Measures:* valid route, extra distance/moves, constraint errors, and completion time.
43. **Pack for the Day** — Select items for a stated situation (rainy walk, picnic, library visit) from useful and irrelevant choices. *Measures:* appropriate selections, omissions, distractor selections, and time.
44. **Make the Schedule** — Place a few familiar activities into a daily timetable given simple rules (breakfast before a walk, appointment at 2). *Measures:* rule satisfaction, placements, corrections, and time.
45. **Build the Budget** — Select items that fit a small stated budget, or choose the correct combination of coins for an amount. *Measures:* calculation accuracy, overspend/underspend, time, and help requests.
46. **Shop and Make Change** — Choose items from a short shopping list and determine simple totals or change. Offer number-pad, coin, or multiple-choice response. *Measures:* correct totals/change, arithmetic steps, and time.
47. **Everyday Estimation** — Choose which container holds more, which route is shorter, or approximately how many objects are present. *Measures:* selection, accuracy range, latency, and item type.
48. **Complete the Rule Grid** — Fill a small grid so each row/column follows a simple icon, color, or number rule. Keep instructions visible. *Measures:* rule consistency, placements, corrections, and time.
49. **Choose the Best Tool** — Match a household job or pictured task with an appropriate tool from a small selection. Later levels add plausible distractors. *Measures:* choice accuracy and response time.
50. **Cause and Effect** — Arrange or match simple picture pairs showing a cause and its likely result (watering a plant → plant grows; ice in sun → melts). *Measures:* relation accuracy, time, and reasoning category.

## E. Language and verbal expression

51. **Name the Picture** — Show a familiar object and ask the player to say its name or choose from options. *Measures:* correct naming, time to first response, skipped items, and answer mode.
52. **Name More in a Category** — Ask the player to name animals, foods, or other familiar things for a short timed or untimed interval. The player can speak or tap pictures. *Measures:* unique items, repeats, pauses, and total time.
53. **Say Words Beginning With…** — Give a starting sound or letter and invite words that begin with it. Offer a picture-based alternative for players who prefer not to speak. *Measures:* valid words, repetitions, latency, and response mode.
54. **What Do These Have in Common?** — Show or say two familiar items and ask for a shared category or property (for example, bike and bus are ways to travel). *Measures:* accepted relation, response latency, and hint use.
55. **Listen and Choose** — Speak a short instruction or description, then let the player select the matching picture. Add a second step in harder rounds. *Measures:* accuracy by step, replay requests, response time, and audio setting.
56. **Describe the Scene** — Show an original picture and invite the player to describe what is happening. The activity can be conversational, with optional caregiver rating or speech-to-text-based item counts. *Measures:* response start time, relevant details, and duration, where the scoring method is clearly recorded.
57. **Find the Word** — Give a description (“something you use to unlock a door”) and choose or say the matching word. *Measures:* accuracy, response time, and clue level.
58. **Complete the Saying** — Finish familiar, locally selected phrases or common sayings, with alternatives for different languages and cultures. *Measures:* completion accuracy, response latency, and whether a hint was used.
**E59. Naming Ladder** — Name increasingly specific or less common pictures, with a choice-based option. Keep word familiarity and language recorded for each form. *Measures:* correct naming, time to first response, circumlocutions, and clue level.

**E60. Name the Action** — Show a person or object doing something and ask for the action word (for example, pouring, folding, or climbing). A related fluency round asks for several action words. *Measures:* valid verbs, pauses, repetitions, and response method.

**E61. Word Relationships** — Match a word with its definition, synonym, antonym, or rhyme using original, familiar content. *Measures:* choice accuracy, response time, and relationship type.

**E62. Build the Sentence** — Arrange word tiles into a meaningful sentence, or choose the grammatically correct sentence from two options. *Measures:* word order, corrections, completion time, and text size setting.

**E63. Read and Understand** — Read a short passage or listen to it read aloud, then answer a simple question. Include a listen-only option and record which mode was used. *Measures:* reading/speech response, comprehension accuracy, latency, and replay count.

**E64. Write or Spell a Word** — Enter a dictated word or spell a familiar word with the touch keyboard or letter tiles. A separate option can ask for a word to be spelled backward. *Measures:* letter accuracy, corrections, time, and input method.

**E65. Conversation Cards** — The app asks simple open questions about a picture, activity, or familiar topic; the player answers aloud. Keep this as a conversational exercise, with optional caregiver notes rather than relying on one automated speech score. *Measures:* response start time, duration, and optional content/fluency observations.

**E66. Read a Short Passage Aloud** — Read a brief, large-print passage on screen, with a listening alternative. *Measures:* completion, optional word-level reading observations, pauses, and duration.

**E67. Rapid Picture Naming** — Name a sequence of familiar colors, objects, or digits at a comfortable pace, with an untimed option. *Measures:* valid names, naming latency, pauses, and repetitions.

**E68. Yes or No?** — Answer simple factual questions (for example, whether a stone usually floats) using large yes/no buttons or speech. Localize content and allow “not sure.” *Measures:* response, latency, and content version.

## F. Numbers and practical calculation

59. **Subtract in Steps** — Start from a number and repeatedly subtract a fixed amount (for example, start at 100 and subtract 7), entering answers on a large keypad. Vary starting values and step size for alternate forms. *Measures:* each answer, error type, inter-response time, and total duration.
60. **Count Back by Ones or Twos** — Speak or tap a descending sequence. Increase span or change step size gradually. *Measures:* sequence accuracy, pauses, and response mode.
61. **Fill the Missing Number** — Choose the number missing from a short ordered sequence. Include counting, alternating, or simple addition patterns. *Measures:* accuracy, response time, and rule type.
62. **Compare the Amounts** — Choose which group has more, fewer, or the same number of objects. Increase the quantities or reduce visual separation. *Measures:* accuracy and latency.
63. **Coin Combinations** — Build an amount by selecting coins or numerals. Use locally appropriate currency and record the currency/content version. *Measures:* exact amount, extra/omitted coins, moves, and time.
64. **Number Facts in Daily Life** — Answer simple questions about time, quantities, or a small receipt. Keep the wording and arithmetic clear, and allow untimed play. *Measures:* correct steps, response time, and help used.

## G. Audio and microphone activities

65. **Spoken Number Echo** — The tablet speaks digits; the player repeats them aloud or taps digits. Offer forward, backward, and reordered modes. *Measures:* longest correct span, order errors, response latency, and input mode.
66. **Listen for a Sound** — Play a mixed sequence of sounds (bell, bird, door, water) and ask the player to tap when a target sound is heard. *Measures:* hits, misses, false alarms, and response times.
67. **Sound Order** — Play several familiar sounds in sequence, then have the player replay their order using sound buttons. *Measures:* span and sequence accuracy.
68. **Echo the Rhythm** — Play a short rhythm and let the player tap it back on large pads. Begin with slow, distinct beats. *Measures:* order, timing deviation, retries, and pace setting.
69. **Spoken Word Recall** — Read a short original list of words, then invite recall after a delay. Use rotating lists with recorded audio versions and a consistent speaking rate. *Measures:* recalled items, intrusions, pauses, and recall latency.
70. **Repeat a Sentence** — Speak a short sentence and ask the player to repeat it. Vary sentence length and structure using original material. *Measures:* words/order retained, response start time, and replay count.
71. **Listen, Remember, Act** — State a simple rule, play a sequence, and ask for a response only to items that meet the rule (such as tap for animals). Later rounds can switch the target category. *Measures:* accuracy, false presses, omissions, and target response time.
72. **Recognize the Everyday Sound** — Play a familiar sound and ask the player to choose its source (kettle, dog, rain, phone). *Measures:* choice and latency.
73. **Follow Spoken Steps** — Give one or two concrete steps (“tap the flower, then the blue cup”). Increase to three steps only at higher levels. *Measures:* step accuracy, order, replay requests, and time.
**G74. Screen Is the Target** — Briefly show a word, picture, or short sequence while the speaker presents different distracting material. Ask the player to recall or select only what appeared on screen—not what was spoken. Rotate which modality is the target in separate versions. *Measures:* screen-target accuracy, spoken-distractor intrusions, response time, presentation speed, stream order, and replay requests.

**G75. Do They Match?** — Present a picture or symbol and play a spoken word or sound. The player responds only when the visual and audio match according to the stated rule. *Measures:* congruent hits, incongruent false alarms, omissions, and response time.

**G76. Listen, Then Sort** — Drag or tap objects while listening for instructions that can change the sorting or placement rule partway through. Repeat directions on request. *Measures:* placement accuracy, rule-switch errors, instruction replays, and time.

**G77. Spoken Orientation** — Ask a simple question about day, season, or current place, with an open spoken answer or multiple-choice tiles. Allow a neutral clue or skip. *Measures:* response, latency, hint use, and answer method.

**G78. Show and Say the Sequence** — Show and speak a sequence, then invite the player to repeat it using both tapping and speech. A simpler form can ask for one mode at a time. *Measures:* sequence accuracy by mode, order errors, response time, and modality setting.

**G79. Listen for a Letter** — Play a stream of spoken letters and tap whenever the target letter is heard. Use a single clear target and adjustable pace. *Measures:* hits, misses, false presses, and target response time.

**G80. Count the Beeps** — Play a short series of beeps or flashes and ask how many occurred. Vary sound and visual versions separately or combine them in a dual-attention round. *Measures:* count accuracy, response time, and modality.

**G81. Say the Months Backward** — Speak the months or days of the week in reverse order, with a tap-choice alternative. *Measures:* sequence accuracy, order errors, pauses, and response mode.

## H. Camera and real-world interaction ideas

Camera activities are optional and exploratory. They should work in a well-lit space, explain what the camera will do, process locally where feasible, and avoid saving images unless the person/caregiver deliberately chooses to save them.

74. **Find Something Nearby** — Ask the player to show a familiar object that matches a prompt (“something used for writing,” “something round”). The camera may suggest a broad match; the player or caregiver confirms it. *Measures:* completion, time, prompt category, and assistance.
75. **Find the Matching Object** — Show a picture on the tablet, then invite the player to locate and show a matching real object. *Measures:* match completion, time, and help used.
76. **Tabletop What Changed?** — With a caregiver's help, place a few objects in camera view, let the player study them, then change one position or remove one item. Ask what changed. *Measures:* recall/recognition accuracy, response time, and set size.
77. **Copy the Arrangement** — Show a simple arrangement of household objects on screen; the player arranges real objects to match while the camera gives optional feedback. *Measures:* placement accuracy, sequence, time, and assistance.
78. **Photo Memory Set** — With permission, use a caregiver-selected photo of a neutral familiar object or place; later ask the player to recognize or recall details. Keep personal photos separate from standard alternate forms. *Measures:* recognized details, delay, and prompt level.
79. **Show Me Something Red** — Ask the player to point the tablet camera at something red. A simple on-device vision check can count the proportion of sampled camera pixels that fall within a configurable red-color range and confirm when enough red is visible. Other colors or simple shapes can be alternate forms. *Measures:* time to detection, red-pixel proportion, detection confidence, and whether the player needed another prompt. Keep the threshold adjustable for lighting and camera conditions; process the preview locally and do not save frames by default.
80. **Copy a Simple Gesture** — The tablet demonstrates a simple hand/arm gesture sequence; the player imitates it while the camera optionally detects completion. Treat movement ability and camera recognition as separate recorded factors. *Measures:* sequence order, completion, retries, and recognition confidence.
**H81. Mirror Body-Parts Game** — In optional self-view mode, ask the player to point to a named feature (nose, ears, shoulders) or make a simple expression. The player can tap the matching labeled image instead of moving. *Measures:* selected target, completion, time, and response method.

**H82. Show the Number** — Ask the player to hold up a requested number of fingers, or show the same number as a large screen numeral. A caregiver can confirm the response if automatic finger counting is unavailable. *Measures:* requested/displayed number, confirmed response, time, and assistance.

**H83. Self-View During a Voice Game** — Optionally show the front-camera self-view while the player names pictures or repeats a sequence, so the activity can feel more face-to-face during a shared session. Treat this as an interaction preference or caregiver observation, not an automatically scored emotion/engagement signal.

**H84. Find a Detail on a Label** — Use the camera on a generic product label or a prepared practice card, then ask the player to locate a large-print date, word, or symbol. An on-screen mock label is the alternative. *Measures:* target found, search time, and assistance.

**H85. Photo Diary Recall** — Optionally take or select a photo from the day, describe it together, and later ask the player to recognize or recall a detail. Keep this personal set separate from standard forms. *Measures:* recalled details, delay, and prompt level.

## I. Combined and social games

81. **Story Builder** — Select a person, place, and object from picture cards, then arrange them into a short story. Can be played together with a family member. *Measures:* selections, sequence, time, and optional engagement rating.
82. **Two-Player Turn-Taking** — Alternate turns matching pairs, continuing a pattern, or naming an item in a category. The app can give each player a clear turn cue. *Measures:* resident's turns, correct actions, response time, and completed rounds.
83. **Remember Our Plan** — Together select a few activities for later in the game; after another activity, ask the player which activity was chosen or what comes next. *Measures:* delayed recall, order, and prompts.
84. **Music and Memory** — Play short, licensed or original instrumental excerpts and ask the player to match the tune, choose an associated image, or remember the order. Use preferences only when deliberately selected. *Measures:* recognition, order, response time, and familiarity rating.
85. **Everyday Scenario Choices** — Present a simple situation (rain before a walk, a letter that needs mailing) and choose a useful next step. Keep choices clear and allow discussion rather than forcing a single response where reasonable alternatives exist. *Measures:* selected choice, time, and optional explanation.

## J. Everyday functional simulations

These activities simulate familiar daily routines using touch controls. Use generic scenarios and practice data; the goal is an enjoyable activity and a record of task performance, not to change a real-world routine.

**J1. Set the Table** — Drag or tap plates, cups, and utensils into a table setting for one or more people. Add a simple rule such as “put a cup beside each plate.” *Measures:* placements, omissions, corrections, and time.

**J2. Make Tea or a Sandwich** — Complete an interactive sequence by selecting ingredients/tools and performing steps in order. This expands the static picture-order task into a multi-step touch simulation. *Measures:* step order, skipped/repeated steps, help, and time.

**J3. Fill the Week Box** — Use colored tokens to place a sample schedule into a weekly organizer. Use fictional symbols and example schedules rather than real medication instructions. *Measures:* day/time placement, omissions, corrections, and time.

**J4. Dial the Number** — Enter a displayed fictional phone number on a large simulated keypad, or choose the correct contact from a short list. *Measures:* digit/order errors, corrections, and time.

**J5. Find the Bill Details** — Review a simple sample bill or receipt and answer questions about its amount, due date, or line item. *Measures:* information located, answer accuracy, and response time.

**J6. Use the Simulated Appliance** — Complete a familiar control task on a mock microwave, thermostat, or TV remote, such as setting a timer or choosing a channel. *Measures:* correct controls, sequence errors, corrections, and time.

**J7. Read the Label** — Find a specified detail on a large mock product label, such as the expiry date or a highlighted instruction. *Measures:* target found, false selections, time, and magnification setting.

**J8. Schedule the Appointment** — Place a fictional appointment on a calendar at a given day and time. Include a “next Friday” variant with an on-screen calendar reference. *Measures:* date/time accuracy, corrections, and completion time.

**J9. Read the Timetable** — Use a simple, clearly labeled bus or event timetable to choose a departure that meets a stated condition. Keep locations and conventions adaptable. *Measures:* selected row/column, accuracy, and time.

**J10. Find the Safety Hazard** — Tap a clearly depicted hazard in a familiar scene, such as a wet floor or an unplugged appliance cord. Keep examples concrete and discussable. *Measures:* hazards found, false alarms, and search time.

**J11. Dress for the Weather** — Choose clothing for a pictured weather condition and put the items into a simple order. Allow culturally and personally appropriate clothing options. *Measures:* relevant selections, sequence, and time.

**J12. Fill in a Sample Form** — Enter fictional information into a short mock form using large fields and a touch keyboard. *Measures:* field completion, entry errors, corrections, and time; do not use real personal details.

## K. Motor, praxis, and touch dexterity

Keep these separate from reasoning/memory outcomes because they also depend on hand comfort, mobility, and familiarity with the device. Offer one-handed, tap-only, and skip options.

**K1. Finger Tap Count** — Tap a large target as quickly and steadily as comfortable for a short interval, then optionally repeat with the other hand. *Measures:* taps, inter-tap timing, missed touches, and hand used.

**K2. Alternating Taps** — Tap two large buttons in alternating order or follow a prompted left-right sequence. *Measures:* sequence accuracy, timing, omissions, and hand/mode used.

**K3. Drag Along the Track** — Move a large token along a broad path without leaving it. Offer no-timer practice. *Measures:* path deviation, boundary touches, completion, and time.

**K4. Pinch or Resize** — Use pinch/spread gestures or plus/minus buttons to resize an object to match a target. *Measures:* final size error, adjustment count, and interaction method.

**K5. Turn the Dial** — Rotate a large on-screen dial to a marked position, with a tap-based version using arrow buttons. *Measures:* final angle/position error, overshoot, corrections, and time.

**K6. Set the Slider** — Move a large slider to a target marker such as a volume level. *Measures:* final distance from target, corrections, and time.

**K7. Draw a Path or Spiral** — Trace a simple line or spiral on screen with finger or stylus. Keep drawing activities optional and offer shape stamps as an alternative. *Measures:* path deviation, pauses, lift-offs, corrections, and completion time.

**K8. Pantomime the Action** — Ask the player to show how they would perform a familiar action, such as brushing teeth, using a comb, or pouring a drink. A caregiver may confirm completion; camera analysis is optional. *Measures:* action sequence, prompts, and response method.

**K9. Guide the Mercury Drop** — Show a large, liquid-looking bubble or droplet in a shallow maze. The player tilts the tablet to guide it along a wide path into a bucket or other clear goal. Start with a short, forgiving route; later versions can add gentle turns, wider/narrower sections, or a few safe obstacles. Calibrate a neutral holding angle at the start, use a comfortable tilt range, and provide a touch-control alternative or skip option. *Measures:* completion, elapsed time, path deviation, wall contacts, pauses, reversals, and tilt range/smoothness. Treat movement data separately from reasoning or memory results because it also depends on hand comfort and ability to hold the device. Use device-orientation sensing (such as Android's rotation-vector or accelerometer data); a gyroscope can supplement motion sensing but should not be the sole source for sustained tilt angle because integrated gyro readings drift.

## L. Social cognition and emotion games

These are optional, culturally adaptable activities. Use clear, non-trick scenarios and record the content set because social conventions and expressions vary.

**L1. Match the Expression** — Match a pictured facial expression to a simple label or situation. Offer a neutral “not sure” option. *Measures:* choice, latency, and scenario version.

**L2. What Might They Feel?** — Match an expression or short scenario with a plausible feeling, allowing more than one reasonable answer when appropriate. *Measures:* selected response, time, and content version.

**L3. Hear the Tone** — Listen to a short acted phrase and choose between broad tone descriptions (for example, cheerful, calm, or upset). *Measures:* choice, latency, and audio version.

**L4. Choose a Helpful Reply** — Select a polite or supportive response in a short everyday interaction. *Measures:* chosen option, response time, and scenario version.

**L5. What Does the Character Know?** — Follow a short story in which one character did not see an object moved, then choose where the character will look. Keep story length and memory load configurable. *Measures:* answer, latency, and story recall details.

**L6. Interpret the Saying** — Match a familiar proverb or nonliteral phrase to a plain-language meaning, with culturally localized forms and a literal-choice distractor. *Measures:* interpretation, response time, language, and phrase version.

## M. Sensory and music activities

These can be enjoyable standalone games and can also help record whether a sensory setting was usable on that day. Keep their results distinct from cognitive-task scores.

**M1. Compare the Tones** — Play two clear tones and ask which is higher or whether they sound the same. *Measures:* choice, latency, and playback level.

**M2. Hum or Sing a Tune** — Play or invite a familiar melody and let the player hum or sing along. Keep this as a music activity; optional pitch/rhythm features should be stored separately from task accuracy. *Measures:* duration, participation, and optional rhythm/pitch match.

**M3. Contrast and Color Check** — Choose a clearly visible symbol or distinguish large color patches at the current display setting. Use this to note display/accessibility conditions, not as a general cognitive result. *Measures:* visibility setting, missed targets, and display configuration.

**M4. Sound Heard?** — Play a quiet tone or everyday sound at a comfortable chosen volume and ask whether it was heard. Allow the user/caregiver to adjust volume and mark headphones/hearing aids if relevant. *Measures:* response and playback setting.

## N. Orientation and time awareness

These activities can use the device's date/time and the person's selected locale, with a clue, skip, or tap-choice response.

**N1. Read the Clock** — Choose or say the time shown on an analog or digital clock. This complements “Set the Clock Hands,” which asks the player to construct a time. *Measures:* time-reading accuracy, response time, and display format.

**N2. Calendar Yesterday/Tomorrow** — Use a calendar to identify yesterday, tomorrow, or the next named weekday. *Measures:* selection accuracy, time, and calendar support used.

**N3. Person and Place Cards** — Match a person's name/relationship or a familiar place to an optional profile card. Allow the family/care team to choose whether personalized prompts are used. *Measures:* response, latency, and cue level.

**N4. Estimate the Wait** — Ask the player to tap when they think a short interval, such as 30 seconds, has passed. *Measures:* elapsed-time error, early/late response, and session context.

**N5. Which Season Fits?** — Match a weather or seasonal scene to a season or likely clothing choice. Localize seasons and accept reasonable regional answers. *Measures:* match, latency, and content version.

## Data to keep for each activity

Each exercise definition should document:

- Stable exercise ID, title, primary/secondary skill tags, and a short description.
- Content set/form ID, language, difficulty parameters, instructions version, and audio/image asset versions.
- Session and trial timestamps; stimulus onset and response time where available.
- Expected and actual response, correctness, misses/false alarms, corrections, retries, hints, and skipped items.
- Response method (tap, drag, voice, camera-assisted, caregiver-assisted), audio playback rate, visual presentation duration, any response deadline (including no deadline), and accessibility settings that affect interaction.
- The event used to start response timing; preserve per-item latency separately from total activity duration.
- Optional note for interruption or unusual session conditions, entered by the player/caregiver rather than inferred.
- Optional, player/caregiver-entered context such as energy, mood, pain, sleep quality, noise, glasses/hearing aids in use, or caregiver help. Keep these items optional and separate from exercise scores.
- Whether media was processed, retained, or discarded; the default should avoid storing raw microphone audio or camera frames.

Store enough detail to understand a person's own results across sessions. A single combined score should not replace the separate measures or the original task/version details.

## Candidate priorities for the first playable suite

To keep the first suite varied but manageable, consider starting with one activity from each of these interaction styles:

1. Picture-pair matching (existing prototype).
2. Pattern continuation (existing prototype).
3. Bicycle structure choice (visual reasoning).
4. Toolbox outline matching (drag/drop and tap alternative).
5. Category target listening with a large response button.
6. Spoken or on-screen number sequence recall.
7. Object-to-use association matching.
8. Visual search / tap-the-target.
9. Screen Is the Target (visual recall with spoken distraction).
